# Categories hold money; budgets are targets

A category is an envelope of real money. It can sit in several accounts and carries over from month to month. Income lands in an account's Unassigned money, and an Allocation moves money between categories inside one account without being a transaction. A transfer moves one category's money between accounts.

A budget stays a monthly target, compared against what was spent. It never goes down as money is spent. This amends the vision's original "not envelope budgeting" line: the household thinks of categories as envelopes inside bank accounts.

Credit cards hold no category money. Each card names a default Paying Account. Recording a card purchase moves the category's money into that card's Card Payment envelope in the paying account, and paying the bill is a transfer out of it. If the bill is paid from another account, the reservation moves first.

The invariant that keeps balances honest: an account's balance always equals its Unassigned plus its category money.
